# A Hilbert-style curve on the spectre tiling

11 November 2026. Arjun, with Claude. `METHODS.md` has the full detail and `EXPLAINER.md` a plain-language version.
This file is ahead of the online working doc, which lacks the better-locality rules and the section on marked tiles.

## Summary

The spectre tiling admits a Hilbert-style curve. For every level of the substitution hierarchy there is a path that
visits every tile of a supertile once, steps only across shared edges, and finishes each sub-supertile before leaving it.

- **Rule.** A recursive rule with 30 states generates the curve at every level.
- **Proof.** A finite certificate, checked in exact arithmetic, shows the rule works at all levels from 2 up.
  Levels 1 to 5 are also settled by exhaustive search.
- **Whole plane.** One state of the rule gives a two-sided infinite curve through every tile of a spectre tiling.
- **Locality.** The rule's worst-case stretch is 8.97984, pinned between 8.979840 and 8.979841 at every level.
  The Hilbert curve scores 6.

Status: computer-assisted. Two programs written in the same session, sharing no code, accept the certificate. Nobody
else has reviewed it. General theory for such curves on self-similar sets exists; a short literature search found no
construction for the spectre.

## Setting

The spectre is the chiral aperiodic monotile of Smith, Myers, Kaplan and Goodman-Strauss. Every spectre tiling has a
hierarchy: tiles group into level-1 supertiles, those into level-2 supertiles, and so on. The construction used here
is the published one, with nine supertile types.

Geometrically there are two shapes. **G** is the Gamma supertile, with 7 children. **D** covers the other eight
types, which share one shape with 8 children. At level 0, D is a single spectre and G is a mystic, a pair of spectres.

A **hierarchical curve** on a level-n supertile is an ordering of its tiles such that

1. every tile appears once,
2. consecutive tiles share an edge,
3. every sub-supertile, at every level below n, occupies one contiguous run.

Condition 3 is what makes the curve Hilbert-like: nearby positions on the curve are nearby in the plane at every scale.

## The rule

The rule has 30 states, 10 for G and 20 for D, built from 12 locations.

- **Location.** A tile named by an eventually periodic word of child indices, read from the top level down.
  `0(5)` means child 0, then child 5 forever. The same word names a tile at every level.
- **State.** A shape with an entry location and an exit location, written `shape|entry|exit`.
- **Production.** An order for the state's children and a state for each child. The first child inherits the parent's
  entry with its first letter dropped, and the last child inherits the exit the same way.

Example: the state `D|(5)|0(5)` enters at the tile addressed 5, 5, 5, ... and leaves at 0, 5, 5, .... Its production
visits the children in the order 5, 6, 7, 4, 3, 2, 1, 0:

| Step | Child | Child state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 | `D\|(5)\|0(5)` |
| 2 | 6 | `D\|(5)\|0(5)` |
| 3 | 7 | `G\|0(5)\|3(2)` |
| 4 | 4 | `D\|6(2)\|(5)` |
| 5 | 3 | `D\|0(5)\|(5)` |
| 6 | 2 | `D\|0(5)\|(7)` |
| 7 | 1 | `D\|(2)\|(5)` |
| 8 | 0 | `D\|0(5)\|(5)` |

This state contains itself twice, so its curve at level n contains its own curve at level n-1. The full rule is in
`results/rule_min30.json`.

## Proof for all levels

Every state of the rule has a hierarchical curve between its entry and exit tiles at every level n >= 2. The proof is
an induction with a finite, machine-checked core.

1. **Base case.** At level 2 (71 or 62 tiles) an exhaustive search finds a hierarchical curve for each of the 30 states.
2. **What the step needs.** A state at level n is its children's curves at level n-1, concatenated. This works if, for
   each consecutive pair of children, the exit tile of the first shares an edge with the entry tile of the second.
   Call this a junction. The rule has 200 junctions, and each must hold at every level.
3. **Junctions descend.** A junction is a triple: the relative position of two supertiles, an exit location and an
   entry location. Dropping the first letter of each location gives the same claim one level down, about the two
   children those letters name. So each triple points to another triple.
4. **Relative positions are exact and finite.** Tile vertices lie in the ring generated by the 12th roots of unity,
   so every position is four integers. A supertile's four key points at level n+1 are a fixed integer-linear function
   of those at level n. Two relative positions that agree at 16 consecutive levels agree at all later levels, which
   gives each one a canonical key. Only 24 relative positions occur.
5. **Closure.** Starting from the 200 junctions, descent reaches 32 distinct triples and then cycles. Each of the 32 is
   checked directly at level 2 in exact arithmetic. By induction on the level, all 32 hold at every level, and so do
   all 200 junctions.

The argument in step 4 rests on one linear-algebra fact: a linear function that vanishes on 16 consecutive iterates of
a 16-dimensional linear recurrence vanishes on all later iterates. The orbit here spans only 6 dimensions.

## From supertiles to the plane

**Any tiling.** Fix a tile and its chain of supertiles S1, S2, S3, .... A state for one supertile fixes, through its
production, a state for the supertile below it. There are finitely many states at each level, so a choice consistent
across all levels exists. The resulting orders agree where they overlap and give one hierarchical curve through the
union of the chain. When that union is the whole plane, the curve visits every tile of the tiling. It is infinite in
one direction or in both.

**An explicit two-sided curve.** The state `D|(5)|0(5)` contains itself as child 6, at step 2 of 8, so tiles
accumulate on both sides of the nested copy. The copy two levels down sits strictly inside: its distance to the
boundary is 2, 4 and 10 tiles at levels 3, 4 and 5. Each pair of levels therefore adds at least one full ring of
tiles, the nested supertiles exhaust the plane, and the curve is a single two-sided path through every tile of that
tiling.

The interior claim is certified for every level from 4 up, in exact arithmetic, and level 3 is checked directly. Call
a supertile surrounded by a set of neighbours if every edge of each of its tiles is shared inside that set. A child is
surrounded by its siblings and the children of its parent's neighbours, so the property descends. The nested copy has
6 neighbours, descent reaches 17 distinct cases, and each holds at level 2.

Some spectre tilings split into several regions that no finite supertile crosses. There the argument gives one curve
per region. Joining them is not addressed.

## Locality

The worst-case stretch of the 30-state rule is 8.97984, against 6 for the Hilbert curve. Stretch is the maximum, over
pairs of tiles, of squared distance divided by tile area times the number of steps between them along the curve.

| Level | Tiles | Worst stretch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 4,401 | 8.07 |
| 5 | 34,649 | 8.48 |
| 6 | 272,791 | 8.69 |
| 7 | 2,147,679 | 8.87 |
| 8 | 16,908,641 | 8.94 |
| limit along the worst family | | 8.980 |

Each value is the exact maximum over all pairs at that level, found by branch and bound. At levels 6, 7 and 8 the
maximum is attained by the same self-similar pair: the tiles at addresses `41(2)` and `440(2)`. Followed to level 40
in exact arithmetic, the ratio along that pair increases to 8.97984.

A second branch and bound gives the matching upper bound at every level. It works on pairs of sub-supertiles: either a
bound on centre distance plus radii, valid at all levels, settles the pair, or the pair is checked exactly at level 2
and split into its children. No pair anywhere exceeds 8.979841, and a target of 8.97984 fails on an actual pair at
level 19. So the constant lies between 8.979840 and 8.979841. This bound runs in double-precision floating point,
treats the normalised geometry as stationary beyond level 45 (it changes by less than 4e-15 there), and has one
implementation.

Sampling about 4,000 other closed rules found some with lower values at levels 4 and 5. The one tested at level 6
came out the same, 8.68 against 8.69, and over all its states that rule exceeds 9.07. A later local search driven by the all-level bound did find better rules: 8.745 with 181 states
(`results/rule_best_locality.json`, accepted by both checkers) and 8.766 with 122 states. The search was cut short, so this is far
from a limit. No lower bound is known beyond 2.84, which comes from the diameter of a supertile.

## Minimality

Thirty states is the smallest closed rule when locations have at most one letter before a single repeating letter. An
exact optimiser (CP-SAT) proves this over the 642 valid states of that family.

| Location family | Boundary locations (G / D) | Valid states | Smallest rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| one repeating letter | 5 / 6 | 0 | none exists |
| repeating pair | 16 / 19 | 0 | none exists |
| one letter, then one repeating letter | 28 / 32 | 642 | 30, proven optimal |
| one letter, then a repeating pair | 100 / 117 | 3,480 | between 28 and 30 |

A valid state is one that survives in the largest self-consistent set: it has a level-2 curve and a production into
other valid states with certified junctions. A smaller rule may exist with longer location words.

## Marked tiles

Record, for each tile, the edge where the curve enters and the edge where it leaves. Call that unordered pair a marking.

- **No single marking can work.** Each edge of a spectre meets three to five different edges of its neighbours, so no
  pair of edges only ever meets itself. Markings using a proper subset of the edges are ruled out the same way.
- **Truchet-style markings fail.** Pairing all 14 edges by seven non-crossing arcs can be done in 429 ways. On the
  559-tile supertile every one produces closed loops, and that supertile occurs in every spectre tiling.
- **Twenty markings suffice for this curve, and 20 is its minimum.** Neighbouring tiles usually share several edges,
  so the crossing edge is a choice at 4,252 of 4,400 steps. An exact solver finds 20 markings and proves that optimal
  on 4,401 tiles. The same 20 suffice on 34,649 tiles.

With the natural drawing rule (through each tile centre, crossing at the middle of each shared border) the same curve
uses 32 markings. Whether another curve needs fewer than 20, and whether the marked tiles force the curve, are open.

## A smaller form: the spectre tree

The 30 states exist because a path has two ends. A tree has none, and the tiling supplies the tree.

- **Corners.** Every cluster has four corner points, the key points of the published construction. The tile at each
  corner has a fixed address: `6(3)`, `(5)`, `(3)`, `0(5)`.
- **Ring.** The 8 pieces of a cluster form a ring 0-1-2-3-4-5-6-7-0. Each piece shares exactly one corner point with
  the next. Checked at levels 1 to 8.
- **Doors.** At each shared corner the two corner tiles share an edge, at every level (8 doors, certified through 8
  closed triples, plus exact checks at levels 1 and 2).

Join each piece to the next through the door at their shared corner. The mystic cluster has no piece 2, so its ring is
already a chain, 3-4-5-6-7-0-1, with nothing to choose. In the full cluster one join next to piece 2 is left open
(1-2 by default), which makes it the same chain with piece 2 attached at one end. Join the two tiles of each mystic.

At every level this is a spanning tree, so the walk around it is one closed, uncrossed loop that passes every tile.
The description needs no states and no addresses: "join neighbouring pieces at the corner they share".

| | Corner tree, open at 1-2 | Corner tree, open at 2-3 | 30-state path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Times a tile is passed | 1 to 3 | 1 to 3 | 1 |
| Arcs per cluster (through level 5) | up to 3 | up to 3 | 1 |
| Tile markings (level 5) | 18 | 22 | 32 |
| Locality, level 4 | 20.1 | 20.2 | 8.07 |

One binary choice remains: which of the two joins beside piece 2 stays open. The earlier tree with hand-picked doors
(13 doors, three kinds, locality 18.5) is superseded and described in `JOURNAL.md` section 18.

## Related work

Deriving space-filling curves from substitution rules is established. A curve specific to the spectre did not turn up
in about ten searches, which is weak evidence of absence. Details and links are in `LITERATURE.md`.

What looks new, if anything: an explicit finite rule at the level of spectre tiles, a certificate for all levels, a
minimal state count, and the measured locality constant. Someone who knows the Rao and Zhang line of work should judge
how much of that is a routine application.

## Verification and reproducibility

Two checkers accept the certificate and report identical counts: 30 states, 200 junctions, 32 closed triples, 24
relative positions.

| Checker | Arithmetic | Base-case search | Replay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `03_verify_rule.py` | integer coordinates over the 12th roots of unity, formal matrices | dynamic program | levels 2 to 5 |
| `independent_check.py` | rationals plus rational multiples of the square root of 3, explicit transforms at 16 levels | plain recursive search | levels 2 to 4 |

The second checker shares no code with the first and uses only the Python standard library. Both were written by the
same author in one session, and both read the substitution from the same published algorithm. An error in that
reading, or in the shared argument of step 4, would pass both. The interior certificate and the locality bound each
have one implementation.
